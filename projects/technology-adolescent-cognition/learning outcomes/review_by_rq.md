## RQ2: What is the effect of media multitasking and technology u...

> What is the effect of media multitasking and technology use on different types of learning outcomes?

**Evidence found in 3/3 sources (100%)**

### Summary of Findings

**Marker et al. (2018) [Source 1]**

The meta-analysis found that general social networking site (SNS) use and SNS use related to multitasking were negatively associated with academic achievement (grades/GPA), although the effects were small. Conversely, SNS use specifically for academic purposes showed a small positive association with academic achievement. The study found no significant relationship between general SNS use and time spent studying.
*Effect size: ρ = -0.07 (general), ρ = -0.10 (multitasking), ρ = 0.08 (academic purposes)*

> The meta-analyses identified small negative effects of ρ^ = − .07, 95% CI [− .12, − .02] for general SNS use and ρ^ = − .10, 95% CI [− .16, − .05] for SNS use related to multitasking.
> - Page 651

**Paterna et al. (2024) [Source 2]**

The study found a small, negative relationship between problematic smartphone use (PSU) and academic achievement. This negative relationship was consistent across various moderators but was significantly stronger (medium-sized) in samples of elementary and middle school students compared to older students.
*Effect size: r = -0.110*

> Results showed a small effect size (r = -0.110), which tended to be larger in samples consisting of students from elementary and middle schools.
> - Page 1 (Abstract)

**Pyhältö et al. (2025) [Source 3]**

The systematic review found that off-task social media and smartphone usage, particularly addiction and multitasking, are generally negatively associated with academic achievement (GPA, grades). While some studies on use intensity showed mixed results, problematic use and multitasking were consistently linked to poorer outcomes.

> Results suggested an interrelation between off-task smartphone and social media use and academic achievement, and the relationships detected were negative in most of the studies reviewed.
> - Page 1

### Summary of Findings

**Pyhältö et al. (2025) [Source 3]**

The review indicates that multitasking and frequent task switching (often driven by off-task smartphone/social media use) negatively impact academic achievement by increasing cognitive load and breaking down concentration. One included study specifically found that multitasking with social media was associated with poor academic performance.
